Two trials running alongside

Diamox
This week, at home or before a short local walk. Not on Devils Slide. Taste your real on-trail drinks — Liquid IV, salt tabs, whatever you plan to carry — while on Diamox. If the drug makes them taste metallic you will drink less, which is the exact opposite of what you need. Far better to discover that this week and swap products than at Trail Camp.
Beet juice
PASSED. No reflux from the beet juice, from cold coffee, or from the 35 mg caffeine gel. Saturday's bottle is a go. If Friday produces reflux or significant gas, SKIP Saturday's bottle. Two inches of ginger per bottle is more than the Sol-Ti that already gave you reflux, and you have said ginger is always a no. Trading a marginal 3–5% oxygen-efficiency gain for reflux at hour two of a twelve-hour hike is a clearly bad deal.

  • The plan is bigger than what the log had. Six of the nine weeks are 16-25 mi. The largest effort recorded so far is San Jacinto at 13.24 mi and 4,590 ft, which ran 11:04. Aug 1 is 16 mi and 4,700 ft — a step up, not a repeat.

That single week tests the three things nothing else does: sleeping high, carrying a full pack, and a big day on legs that already did a big day. If any week has to survive a schedule cut, it is this one.
  • There is no recovery week between Weeks 3 and 7. Aug 15, 22, 29, Sep 5 and Sep 12 are consecutive weekends of 18, 16, 19, 12 and 25 mi. Week 9 is the only taper. Worth watching resting heart rate and sleep across that block rather than assuming it absorbs cleanly.

Sodium, not water concentration, was the gap. On San Jacinto ~1,900 mg sodium replaced vs ~7,000-9,000 mg lost. Peeing 4x while dehydrated points at LOW SODIUM, not excess water — kidneys dump water they can't hold.

Drinking large volumes at rest stops outpaces absorption. Sip small and frequent; alternate plain and electrolyte bottles. Front-load solid food in hours 1-4 so gels are not doing rescue work at hour 8. Mix through the middle, gels freely on descent. Gels were what made the San Jacinto descent survivable. Hose freezing — the October risk

  • Blow air back down the hose after every sip so no water sits in the line.
  • Insulated hose sleeve, or run the hose inside a jacket layer.
  • Simplest option: use insulated bottles rather than the bladder on summit morning.
  • Sleep with the bladder and bottles inside the tent, not in the vestibule.

What this means for Trail Camp. This is the sharpest argument yet for arriving at Trail Camp with reserves. Getting data out of Garmin

  1. Single activity: connect.garmin.com on the web, open the activity itself, gear icon, Export File. Not available in the mobile app.
  2. Whole history: Account Settings, Data Management, Export Your Data. The activity summary lives at DI_CONNECT/DI-Connect-Fitness/*_summarizedActivities.json.
  3. Strava also offers Export Original on any synced activity, and behaves better on a phone.

Watch settings worth changing

  1. Leave the watch running through breaks. Stopped time is data, and elapsed time is the correct denominator for the fuel budget.
  2. Enable Auto Lap by distance at 1 mile — per-mile splits instead of one undivided lap.
  3. Press lap once at the summit to split ascent from descent exactly.
